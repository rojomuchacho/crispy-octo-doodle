Clock Lab

Can you kneel it out? Set the situation and see the clock play out, snap by snap.

Assumes a 40-second play clock, ~2 seconds per kneel, and that the defense calls a timeout after every kneel while it has one. The math is the same in the NFL and college: both use a 40-second play clock and a two-minute stoppage.

Cheat Sheet

College rules that change decisions

  • First downs: the clock keeps running after a first down, except in the last 2:00 of each half, when it stops until the chains are set (since 2023).
  • Two-minute timeout: college has had an automatic stoppage at 2:00 of each half since 2024, like the NFL's two-minute warning.
  • Tries: snapped from the 3-yard line, for both the PAT kick and a 2-point try (NFL: kick from the 15, 2-point try from the 2).
  • Overtime: each team gets a possession from the opponent's 25, with no game clock. Order flips each OT, and each team gets 1 timeout per OT.
  • 2nd OT: a TD must be followed by a 2-point try. 3rd OT+: teams alternate single 2-point plays.
  • 10-second runoff: applies in the final minute of each half when a foul stops the clock.

NFL overtime

  • Regular season: one 10-minute period; since 2025 both teams are guaranteed a possession. Each team gets 2 timeouts.
  • After both teams have had the ball, the next score wins. Still tied at the end of the period, it's a tie.

NFL clock rules

  • Incomplete pass / out of bounds: clock stops. Outside the last 2:00 of the 1st half and last 5:00 of the 2nd, out-of-bounds restarts on the ready-for-play signal.
  • First down: in the NFL the clock does not stop for a first down (unlike college).
  • Play clock: 40 seconds after a play ends; 25 after a timeout or administrative stoppage.
  • Headset cutoff: coach-to-player radio shuts off with 15 seconds on the play clock (college uses the same cutoff since adding helmet radios in 2024).
  • Two-minute warning: automatic stoppage at 2:00 of each half — a free timeout.
  • 10-second runoff: inside the final minute of a half, an offensive foul that stops the clock lets the defense run 10 seconds off. A timeout avoids it.
  • Spike: stops the clock but costs a down. Never spike on 4th down.

Kneel math

Clock running, each down burns ~42 seconds (40-sec play clock + kneel). Each opponent timeout takes ~40 seconds back.

4th-down rules of thumb

  • 4th & 1–2 almost anywhere past your own 30: go.
  • Opponent's 30–40 ("no man's land"): lean go — the punt nets little and the kick is long.
  • 4th & goal at the 1–2: go, even early.
  • Long yardage in your own territory: punt. Aggressive ≠ correct.
